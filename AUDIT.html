major SITE-01 11 pages show real content but answer 404: the site is served through its error page

Why it matters. A visitor sees each page, but search engines, link checkers and monitoring trust the status: they treat these pages as missing, so they may never appear in search results, and every check calls them broken.

Likely fix. Serve each page with status 200: a single-page app behind a CDN usually needs its error response (the one that returns index.html) set to 200, or each route pre-rendered as its own file.

major SITE-06 1 of the 13 addresses spiralmm.xyz points to does not answer: about 1 connection in 13 can fail

Why it matters. Each new visit picks one of the addresses. On one that does not answer, the page hangs and fails after a long wait. Chrome often tries the next address, so the site looks fine from a browser; scripts, some phones, monitoring and test tools do not.

Likely fix. Remove the address that does not answer from the domain's DNS records (often a registrar's parking or redirect record left beside the hosting's), or make it serve the site.

Why it matters. Every click on one of these links costs an extra round trip before the page starts loading, and search engines see one page at two addresses.

minor SITE-24 4 security headers no page sends

Why it matters. Each header closes a door attackers use (a downgrade to http, a script slipped in, the site framed in another to trick a click). Security reviews and compliance checklists ask for them, and they cost a line of server configuration.

Likely fix. Add the missing headers in the web server or the CDN (one setting each), starting with Strict-Transport-Security.

  • / no page of the site sends Strict-Transport-Security (browsers keep to https)
  • / no page of the site sends Content-Security-Policy (which scripts may run)
  • / no page of the site sends X-Frame-Options or frame-ancestors (another site cannot frame it to trick a click)
  • / no page of the site sends X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff

minor SITE-25 The server says which software and version it runs (AmazonS3)

Why it matters. A version number tells an attacker which known holes to try, without having to look; it helps no visitor.

Likely fix. Turn off the version in the server's configuration (nginx: server_tokens off; Apache: ServerTokens Prod; PHP: expose_php = Off).

minor SITE-28 The site has no sitemap search engines can read

Why it matters. A sitemap tells search engines every page to index, and how fresh each is; without one they find pages only by following links, and one listing wrong addresses spends their visits on errors.

Likely fix. Publish /sitemap.xml listing each page's final address (most site builders and CMSs have a setting or plugin for it), and name it in robots.txt (Sitemap: ...).

minor SITE-29 The site has no security.txt: someone who finds a hole has no way to report it

Why it matters. Researchers who find a security problem look for /.well-known/security.txt to know whom to tell (RFC 9116); without it they may not report it, or report it publicly.

Likely fix. Publish /.well-known/security.txt with a Contact line (an email address or a form) and an Expires line.

minor SITE-31 The site answers at two names, spiralmm.xyz and www.spiralmm.xyz, neither sending visitors to the other

Why it matters. Search engines see every page twice, at two addresses, and split its ranking between them; links and shares point at both.

Likely fix. Pick one name and redirect the other to it with a 301 (one setting in most hosts and CDNs): https://www.spiralmm.xyz/ to https://spiralmm.xyz/, say.
